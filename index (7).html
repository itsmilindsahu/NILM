From opt-in
to infrastructure.

Why appliance-level AI feedback beats every other form of energy billing — and a regulatory blueprint for putting it inside every smart meter in the country.

Milind Sahu — Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Tirupati · Article NSRI-RA-2026-0099 · published 9 June 2026

Read the paper Jump to the numbers ↓
Six months of billing data tells you almost nothing. This paper is about what happens once you tell people which appliance is costing them money.
the problem

The granularity gap

India has installed roughly 250 million smart meters. Almost none of that data ever reaches the people paying the bill in a form they can act on.

Standard billing infrastructure delivers only an aggregate monthly total — and behavioural research says that's not enough information to sustain any corrective action past the first month or two.

the evidence base

Three sources, one question

Does continuous, appliance-level feedback actually change behaviour for longer than a novelty period? The paper triangulates three independent bodies of evidence.

Meta-analysis

Ehrhardt-Martinez et al. — 57 residential feedback studies spanning 12 countries, synthesised for effect size on consumption.

Field trials

Two independent, published longitudinal trials tracking real household behaviour over 12–18 months, not just a post-survey.

Original pipeline

A five-model NILM inference pipeline, evaluated on an 8,000-row synthetic UKDALE-style set with an 80/20 chronological split.

how it works

Five models, one signature

The pipeline runs entirely server-side on data the smart meter already sends — no new household hardware, no sensor to install. It turns one aggregate load curve into a per-appliance signature.

The classifier identifies which appliance is running, the regressor sizes its load, the anomaly detector flags faults, the HMM tracks regime shifts, and the LSTM forecasts what comes next — five specialised models instead of one that tries to do everything.

the proposal

The Civic Energy Disaggregation Mandate

A regulatory framework requiring electricity distribution companies to embed a standardised NILM engine into the smart meter pipelines they already operate, and to surface appliance-level feedback passively through the billing portal every metered customer already opens.

No app to download, no device to install, no opt-in step to abandon — the feedback simply arrives where the bill already lands.

still to do

Where this goes next

  • Compile a publicly available Indian domestic appliance electricity dataset, so models can be benchmarked under heterogeneous load conditions.
  • Develop federated learning architectures for privacy-preserving inference — no raw load data leaving the meter.
  • Run longitudinal field validation in South Asian urban and peri-urban households.
  • Build econometric models linking NILM deployment scale to grid-level peak demand reduction and renewable integration capacity.
